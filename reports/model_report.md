# Model report: Toronto Island Ferry Crowd Forecaster

_Milestone 2. Backtest on the held-out 2024 and 2025 seasons and 2026 to date (through 2026-11-15).
All times are Toronto time (EST/EDT). Every number below comes from
[`model_report_tables.md`](model_report_tables.md), regenerated by
`python -m model.backtest && python -m reports.make_model_report`._

## The short answer

**The model clearly beats every simple baseline, in every slice we measured, including when it uses
the real weather forecasts people would actually have seen.** It's good enough to show the public,
as long as the site keeps showing uncertainty and is honest that a day 4-7 forecast is a rough guess.

| Hourly boardings, 06:00-23:59 (WAPE, lower is better) | LightGBM | best baseline (climatology) | error reduction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Real archived forecasts, 0-1 days ahead | **0.240** | 0.379 | 37% |
| Real archived forecasts, 2-3 days ahead | **0.268** | 0.379 | 29% |
| Real archived forecasts, 4-7 days ahead | **0.309** | 0.379 | 18% |
| Perfect weather (observed), any lead | 0.229-0.231 | 0.380 | 40% |

In everyday terms, from a forecast made the day before:

* Half of all days land within **13.5%** of the actual day total, and 75% within 25%. Six days out the
  typical miss is 20%.
* In summer, the predicted busiest hour is within an hour of the actual one on **70%** of days.
  The hour we recommend as quietest (between 09:00 and 18:59) is among the 3 actually quietest
  hours on **91%** of summer days.
* The busyness level (share of ferry capacity, see below) is exactly right for **90%** of hours
  (climatology: 85%) and within one level for 99.5%.

WAPE = total absolute error / total actual boardings, so 0.24 means hour-by-hour errors add up to
24% of the people who travelled. Single hours are noisy (a group of 300, a boat running late);
day totals and the shape of the day are more reliable than any one hour.

## How it was tested

* **Forecast protocol.** A forecast is issued at 06:00 on day D for D+0 to D+7, hours 06-23. It uses
  only what was known then: ferry counts up to the end of D-1, overnight online sales (00:00-06:00 on D),
  the calendar, holiday history from earlier years, and weather. An automated test checks that
  changing future counts doesn't change any feature.
* **Rolling origin, nothing shuffled.** Train on everything up to 31 Dec 2023 and test 2024; then up to
  2024 and test 2025; then up to 2025 and test 2026 to date. Model settings (Tweedie objective,
  63 leaves, 700 trees) were chosen on a 2023 validation year before the holdout was touched. Quarterly
  refits, closer to production's weekly retraining, gave identical accuracy.
* **Two weather modes, same model:** (a) **observed** weather at Billy Bishop, the ceiling of a perfect
  forecast; (b) **archived real forecasts** from Open-Meteo's Previous Runs API (from Feb 2024, covering
  the whole holdout). For a target on D+lead we use the forecast issued at least (lead+1) x 24 h before
  the valid time, which is always *older* than what a user at 06:00 would see, so (b) is slightly pessimistic.
* **Baselines:** seasonal naive (same weekday and hour, mean of the previous 4 weeks available at issue
  time); same day last year, weekday-aligned; climatology (mean by ISO week x weekday x hour over training years).
* **Data handling** ([`data_quality.md`](data_quality.md)): anomaly periods (2015 roll-out, 2017 flood,
  2020-21 pandemic) and outage days are excluded. Intra-day feed outages followed by a backlog upload are
  **refilled**: the backlog is spread back over the outage hours (3 days since 2015-07, including
  2026-08-22). Outages without a backlog stay missing. Batch transactions (35 isolated quarter-hours of
  1,200-7,200 "boardings") are kept out, because the evidence says they are *extra* transactions, not delayed
  boardings: without them those days are exactly normal (median 0.99 x usual). With them they would
  be 1.39 x usual, e.g. 2,854 at once on a November day when 819 other people travelled.
  Raw values are never deleted (kept in `red_raw` and in the raw archive).

**What changed after we looked at the holdout** (disclosed so you can judge the numbers): the outage
refill (data cleaning), split-conformal interval recalibration (evaluated only on years after the
calibration data), starting the same-day adjustment at noon, the busyness definition (a usability
choice, not accuracy), and the holiday-history features (requested; chosen on 2019/2023 validation
results). Two level-bias fixes were tested **only on pre-holdout validation years** and rejected.

## Where accuracy comes from

**Weather matters most.** On the 2023 validation year, removing all weather features takes hourly
WAPE from 0.231 to 0.370, no better than climatology. Recent-history features carry the level; weather
moves it day to day. So the honest number depends on weather-forecast skill:

| | 0-1 days | 2-3 days | 4-7 days |
|---|---|---|---|
| observed weather (ceiling) | 0.229 | 0.230 | 0.230 |
| real forecasts | 0.240 | 0.268 | 0.309 |
| gap due to weather-forecast error | +0.011 | +0.038 | +0.079 |

**Training on the forecasts people actually get (added Nov 2026).** A model trained only on observed
weather trusts a 6-day forecast as much as a measurement. Archived real forecasts exist from Feb 2024, so
training now includes a second copy of every 2024+ target whose weather is the forecast that was
available at that lead time. With the lead-time feature, the model learns how much weather is worth at
each lead. Tested rolling (2025 trained on 2024's forecast rows; 2026 on 2024-25's): hourly WAPE fell
from 0.264 to 0.251 in 2025 and from 0.312 to 0.307 in 2026, better at every lead time, and the low
bias shrank (2026 off-season -19% to -10%; 2025 off-season -9% to -2%). Giving those rows 3x or 6x
weight was worse. **This choice was made on the 2025-26 holdout itself**, because forecasts don't exist
for earlier years; the 2024 fold is unaffected (0.287) and is the cleanest number here.

**Temperature in context.** Two features compare the day's high with the seasonal normal and with the
previous two weeks (the first warm day of spring draws a crowd that the same temperature in July does
not). On the pre-holdout validation years: 2019 0.291 to 0.285, 2023 0.235 to 0.234. Also tested and
**not adopted** (no consistent gain): the neighbouring days' weather, daylight and hours to sunset, a
slower learning rate, and a trailing bias correction (it halves the bias but makes day totals worse).

**Choosing the weather model by measurement paid off.** Scored against Billy Bishop observations
([`weather_model_selection.md`](weather_model_selection.md)), ECMWF IFS 0.25 deg had the *worst*
temperature error near the terminal (2.1 C at <= 24 h against 1.5 C for GFS). Its nearest grid point is
14 km inland. At every lead, the mean of 2-3 models beat any single model. HRDPS and ECMWF IFS 9 km
are only in the archive from late 2024 / late 2025, for short leads, so they couldn't be compared fairly yet.

**Holidays and events use their own track record.** Every date gets an occasion key: the holiday
itself (`canada_day`, `civic`, ...), the other days of that holiday's long weekend (`civic_lw`), or a
recurring event series from `config/events.csv`. For each earlier occurrence (at least 180 days before
the target, so always known at forecast time) we measure how busy it was against normal same-weekday days
and normal weekend days within +-3 weeks. The model sees the average of the last three occurrences,
and that ratio times the current weekend and weekday level (an "expected total"). Examples:
Victoria Day typically runs 4-5 x a normal Monday; Canada Day about 2 x a weekday, or 1.1-1.3 x a weekend
day. On the pre-holdout validation years this cut the error on holiday days (day-total MAPE 22.7% to
21.6% in 2019, 24.7% to 22.6% in 2023). Overall hourly WAPE was mixed (2023 0.235 to 0.229; 2019 0.288 to 0.296).
It did **not** fix Canada Day 2025, which fell on a Tuesday (see below).

**Trends over years, not just averages.** The model also sees a multi-year trend projection for
every target date and hour. For the same weekday-aligned weeks in each of the last 6 normal years
(pandemic and flood years skipped), it fits a recency-weighted log-linear trend through the yearly
values and extrapolates it to the target year, together with the growth rate and 90-day
year-over-year momentum. On the pre-holdout validation years this cut the bias: 2019 overall from +3.6% to +2.4%
and peak season from +8.8% to +5.6%; 2023 overall from -5.2% to -3.7% and peak from -2.8% to -1.6%. Overall error improved in 2019
(0.296 to 0.289) and slipped in 2023 (0.229 to 0.235). **On the 2024-26 holdout it did not remove the
under-prediction of the 2025-26 surge** (see "Where it fails"): ridership was flat for years and then
jumped about 10% a year, which no trend through past years anticipates. Recency-weighted training was also
tested on validation years and rejected (no better).

## Busyness levels: based on ferry capacity

The spec defined levels from percentiles of the same season and hour. Built that way, 52 boardings at
7 pm in November came out "Very busy" while summer middays carry 2,000-3,500 an hour. Per your
direction, busyness is now **boardings as a share of what the ferries can carry that hour**
(`config/capacity.json`):

* **Vessels** (City of Toronto): Sam McBride 908, Thomas Rennie 908, William Inglis 395, Ongiara 216
  passengers. The new electric ferries (*Lady of the Quays*, *Toronto Islander*, up to 1,300 each) are
  due in service in 2027. Update the config then.
* **Schedule periods** (City schedule page): winter (Oct 14 - Apr 14) is Ward's Island only, ~25
  departures a day; spring and fall have reduced trips on all routes; in summer Centre Island runs every
  15-40 minutes. Nominal capacity = vessels x departures per hour; practical = 80% of nominal.
* **Check against the data:** the busiest hour ever recorded is 3,737 boardings (2026-07-11 13:00),
  96% of peak-summer practical capacity. Each period's observed ceiling sits below its practical capacity.
* **Levels** (share of practical capacity): Quiet < 20% (plenty of room); Moderate 20-40% (steady, still
  walk-on); Busy 40-65% (lines at the gates, boats filling); Very busy >= 65% (boats near full, you may
  wait for the next one). In peak summer that is Busy from ~1,550/h and Very busy from ~2,520/h, where the
  Centre Island boats are near-full, matching reports of 30-60 minute waits on summer weekends.
* **Disruptions** are dated overrides: e.g. with Sam McBride out of service after the 2022-08-20 dock crash,
  the same crowd counts as busier.

| level accuracy, real forecasts, 0-1 days | exact | within one level | climatology, exact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **capacity-based (default)** | **90%** | 99.5% | 85% |
| one year-round percentile scale | 83% | 99.9% | 75% |
| per season and hour (original spec) | 59% | 96% | 45% |

Under the capacity definition most hours are Quiet (65% of peak-summer hours; 88-90% in winter and
spring), so "exact" is flattered by easy hours. The hard part is the busy end: of hours that were actually
Busy, 49% were forecast Busy and 38% one level lower. Of the rare Very busy hours (about 1% of hours), 30% were
forecast Very busy and 50% Busy. That is the usual pull of a point forecast towards the middle. The
likely-range band on the site shows when Very busy is plausible.

## Uncertainty: calibrated ranges

The quantile models are trained on observed weather, so with real forecasts their raw 10-90% range was
**too narrow** (it caught 58-70% of hours instead of 80%). Fix: split-conformal recalibration per (lead day,
peak season), using only *earlier* years' errors. On years the calibration never saw:

| | 0-1 days | 2-3 days | 4-7 days |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2025, off-season | 84% | 85% | 86% |
| 2025, peak | 86% | 87% | 82% |
| 2026, off-season | 81% | 80% | 80% |
| 2026, peak | 75% | 76% | 77% |

2026 is close to the 80% target (a little low in summer, the record year). 2025 runs wide because it is
calibrated on 2024, before the model had any forecast-weather training rows; production calibrates on
all three years.
Day-total ranges on the site use the empirical 10th/90th percentile of day-total errors by lead
(day ahead: -18% / +51%; asymmetric because surprise days are big on the upside).

## Where it fails

**1. Weather-forecast misses dominate the worst days.** About half of the 20 worst day-ahead forecasts
are days when the forecast called for rain that didn't come, or the reverse. With observed weather
the model was close:

* 2026-07-26 (Sun): 5.1 mm forecast, none fell. Predicted 5,200; actual 15,100; with observed weather 16,400.
* 2026-08-10 and 2026-08-16: same story.
* 2024-07-15: the July 2024 flash-flood storm, 34 mm observed against 1.9 mm forecast. Predicted 9,000; actual 3,600.
* 2026-08-22: an evening thunderstorm. Predicted 16,100; actual 9,200; with observed weather 11,300.

**2. Record days are under-predicted, even with perfect weather.** Ridership grew about 10% a year in
2025 and 2026. The model forecasts roughly last year's level plus the recent trend. It was 7-10% low in the
2025-26 peak seasons and 10% low in the 2026 off-season (+1% in 2024). The biggest days were the most
under-predicted: late-July and August 2026 Saturdays (24,000 actual against ~17,000 with perfect
weather). Fixes tried on validation years only (a level offset, a year-over-year growth feature, a
trailing bias correction) didn't help, and more frequent retraining doesn't either. **This is the main thing to watch in
the live accuracy log.** If it persists into 2027, try a recency-weighted training sample.

**3. Holidays that fall on unusual weekdays.** Canada Day 2025 (a Tuesday): predicted 10,300 with
perfect weather; actual 18,600. Earlier Canada Days fell on a Friday, Saturday and Monday, and the model
still leans on "Tuesday". Civic Monday 2026 was a record 27,900 (predicted 19,900-22,000).

**4. Unrecorded events.** A few big misses have no weather explanation (2025-07-12: 24,300 on a
perfect Saturday against 16,200 predicted; 2024-07-28 over-predicted by 4,000). Adding recurring Island events with a `series`
name to `config/events.csv` gives them a track record the model can use after a season or two.

**5. What the data can't see:** queue length, cancellations, routes and return trips. Busyness
compares boardings with scheduled capacity; it is a proxy for lines, not a measurement of them.

## Months ahead: the long-range outlook

A separate model (`model/outlook.py`, report: [`outlook_report.md`](outlook_report.md)) forecasts
daily totals 8-180 days ahead from what is knowable that far out: weekday, season, school breaks,
holidays and their history, last year's same days, year-over-year momentum, and the multi-year trend
projection. It models deviations from that trend projection; a plain model was more biased. Days are
split into hours with the date's projected hourly profile.

On the 2024-26 holdout (issue dates inside each test year), day-total WAPE is about **0.30 in peak
season** and 0.30-0.34 off-season, level with or slightly better than climatology. MAPE is about 0.41 against 0.41-0.43. Without
weather, single days vary a lot: the 10-90% range is roughly 0.55x to 1.7x the forecast. Like the
hourly model, it was unbiased in 2024 and ran 12-15% low in the 2025-26 surge. On the site this is
presented as "typical for the date", with ranges, not as a weather forecast.

## Same-day updates

Today's remaining hours are rescaled by how today has gone so far (shrunk towards 1). Holdout WAPE for
the rest of the day: unadjusted 0.226 / 0.229 / 0.242 / 0.259 at 10:00 / 12:00 / 14:00 / 16:00;
adjusted 0.235 (worse) / **0.208** / **0.212** / **0.230**. So the adjustment switches on once
06:00-11:59 have been counted.

## Is it good enough to show the public?

**Yes**, with the presentation choices built into the site:

* It cuts the error of naive approaches ("like the last few weeks", "like last year") by 35-40%,
  and is a third better than a well-built climatology one day out.
* Days 0-3 are good. Days 4-7 are still clearly better than any baseline, but only modestly better than
  climatology, so the site marks them as "rough guess" with wider ranges.
* The uncertainty band is calibrated, so the "likely range" means what it says.
* The known weakness (under-predicting record days) errs towards "it'll be fine" on the busiest days.
  The likely-range band and live accuracy log keep that visible.

## Decisions for you

1. **Capacity assumptions:** `config/capacity.json` uses the City's vessel capacities and estimated
   departures per hour. If you have the actual current summer timetable, plugging in exact departures would
   sharpen the levels. **Update it when the electric ferries arrive (2027).**
2. **Events:** add recurring Island events with a `series` name (dragon boat festival, Island-wide
   festivals, past disruptions). It's the cheapest accuracy gain available.
3. **Growth bias:** accept and monitor (recommended), or invest in a recency-weighted model before launch?
